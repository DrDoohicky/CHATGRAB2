# ChatGrab workflow explainers — first narration draft

Each script is about one minute at a conversational pace (roughly 120–140 words per minute). Time the recorded voice and adjust pauses in the edit. Use the current app in preview for screen recordings. Highlight selected controls; do not simulate automatic uploads or AI replies. Use the approved official wordmark and existing illustrations. Show example conversations marked “Example”; keep personal data out of recordings.

## 1. Capture — keep the conversation, not just the answer

**Narration:**

“An AI conversation can contain more than an answer. It can hold your goals, the options you considered, and the reasons you chose one over another. Capture saves the messages you choose so you can return to that discussion later. In ChatGrab, open Capture. Paste your conversation text or import a supported file. Before saving, check who said what, the message order, and that the details you need are included. You can also try a public share link. If it brings in only a preview, paste or import the actual messages instead. Imagine planning a trip: the destination matters, but so do the budget and accessibility needs behind it. Save those details together. You now have an original conversation to search, review, and build on.”

**Visual direction:** 0–12s: example trip chat with budget and access requirements highlighted. 12–35s: Capture → paste/import → review speakers and complete text → save. 35–50s: show share-link preview with paste/import alternative. 50–60s: reopen the saved conversation.

**On-screen text:** “Save the messages and the reasons.” / “Check before saving.”

## 2. Provenance — know where an idea came from

**Narration:**

“Where did that decision come from? Provenance is the name for keeping track of a source. In ChatGrab, your original messages remain separate from the notes and AI reviews you add later. That matters because a summary can change the meaning of a discussion. ‘We considered the cheaper option’ is different from ‘we chose it.’ Open the original conversation and select the messages you want to review. Prepare your review request, bring the answer back, and save it as a separate record. Its source references help you return to the messages behind the conclusion. Read those messages before relying on the interpretation. You can keep a useful explanation without losing the conversation that produced it—and revisit the reasoning whenever a question comes up.”

**Visual direction:** 0–15s: compare the two example statements. 15–35s: show original messages beside a saved review, visibly separate. 35–50s: open a source reference and read surrounding messages. 50–60s: show both records retained.

**On-screen text:** “Provenance = where it came from.” / “Original messages ≠ interpretation.”

## 3. Contextual analysis — explore the what and the why

**Narration:**

“Why did the plan change? What are we still waiting for? Contextual analysis helps you explore questions like these across saved conversations. Context means the background that gives a message its meaning: your goal, the limits you face, and the reasons behind a decision. Choose the conversations and passages you want to examine. Then select the questions you want explored. ChatGrab prepares a request containing that material. Review it for private details, copy it into your own ChatGPT account, and paste the answer back into ChatGrab. Compare its explanation with the original messages before saving a reviewed version. You can prepare a Gemini second opinion too. Another opinion is something to examine, not a guarantee. You choose the sources, ask the question, and decide what the answer supports.”

**Visual direction:** 0–15s: highlight goal, limit and reason in an example chat. 15–35s: select passages and analysis types; review prepared request. 35–48s: explicitly show manual copy → ChatGPT → paste back. 48–60s: compare answer and source; save review.

**On-screen text:** “Context = goals + reasons + limits + changes.” / “Choose. Ask. Compare. Review.”

## 4. Evolution — follow how an idea changed

**Narration:**

“An earlier answer and a later answer may look contradictory. Sometimes one is wrong. Sometimes the situation changed. Evolution means following how an idea developed, including the reasons for each change. Suppose your project began with a Friday deadline. Later, the work grew and you agreed to deliver on Monday. Carrying only the Friday date into a new AI chat could send the work in the wrong direction. Find both discussions in ChatGrab and read the messages around the dates. Include them in a review request if you want help explaining the change. Compare the review with those sources, then save the version you accept. Earlier reviews remain available. You keep the history while making it clearer which decision is current and why it replaced the earlier one.”

**Visual direction:** 0–20s: example Friday → expanded scope → Monday timeline. 20–40s: search saved messages and read the earlier/later passages. 40–55s: compare saved review versions. 55–60s: highlight current decision and its reason.

**On-screen text:** “What changed?” / “Why did it change?” / “Which decision applies now?”

## 5. Evidence — check what supports a claim

**Narration:**

“An AI says the feature is finished. A picture shows a button. But does the button work? Evidence is the material that helps you check a claim. A proposed design, a saved file, and an observed result tell you different things. In ChatGrab, attach relevant documents, screenshots or other supporting files to a conversation. Keep them separate from the original messages so you know what was said and what was added later. When reviewing an answer, open its source messages and the files that support it. Record what you checked and what still needs a test. A matching quote shows that the words came from the source; it does not prove the explanation is correct. Keeping these pieces together helps you make a better-informed decision about what to trust next.”

**Visual direction:** 0–15s: “Done” message → design image → actual button test, clearly distinguished. 15–35s: attach a file and open it beside the discussion. 35–50s: read a quoted source and add review notes. 50–60s: show “checked” versus “needs a test.”

**On-screen text:** “Claim. Supporting material. Observed result.” / “A quote is not the whole conclusion.”

## 6. Reintegration — bring the reasons into the next conversation

**Narration:**

“Reintegrate means bringing useful material back into active work. In an AI conversation, that includes more than the final answer. Context is the background that explains it: the goal, the reasons, the limits, and later corrections. An AI may retain a conclusion without all the reasons behind it. In ChatGrab’s Workspace, select the messages you want to reuse, choose a destination, and explain why they matter. Review disagreements, then save a separate continuity record. That is your collection for carrying the work forward. Open its ChatGPT packet, read it, and copy it into a new chat or attach its Markdown file. Ask the AI to restate the decisions and reasons; correct anything it missed. You supply the background, check the response, and keep the originals intact.”

**Visual direction:** 0–15s: venue choice plus low-noise requirement from guide example. 15–35s: select messages → destination → purpose → review disagreements → save. 35–50s: open packet; explicitly show manual copy/attach into a new chat. 50–60s: compare AI restatement against original reasons.

**On-screen text:** “Reintegrate = bring it back into work.” / “Keep the why with the what.” / “You choose what the AI receives.”

## 7. Utility — use what you already worked out

**Narration:**

“You have already spent time thinking with an AI. How do you make that work useful again? Utility means putting saved conversations to practical use. Group related chats in library folders. Search for the discussions behind your decisions. Select the goals, reasons and open questions you need, then prepare a reviewed collection for your next conversation or a handoff to another person. Imagine drafting a project update: instead of starting from memory, bring together what was agreed, why it changed, and what still needs attention. Copy or export that material into the next step, and check the result. Keep a complete backup so your saved library and reviews can be restored later. Project-specific rules and automatic cross-device continuity are in development. Today, you choose the material and direct how it is reused.”

**Visual direction:** 0–20s: library folders and search. 20–40s: select example project decisions, reasons and open questions; prepare packet. 40–50s: manual handoff into a draft project update. 50–60s: complete backup download, then separate “In development” card for project rules and cross-device continuity.

**On-screen text:** “Find it. Understand it. Reuse it.” / “Back up your library.” / “In development: project rules · automatic cross-device continuity.”
